### Assignment: Poster

**Due Thursday, Oct 1**

Make a poster. It can be about anything — an event, a comic, a game, a product, a business. 

***Requirements***
- 1080 × 1350 px (Instagram's portrait post size)
- Must contain at least 140 characters of text
- Can contain some kind of photography or imagery, but it's not required. The focus is on the text
- Export your frame PNG or JPEGs, and submit it along with a link to your Figma

*Examples*
- Remix an album cover
- Promote a concert
- Protest flyer
- Game or movie cover

*Submit via Brightspace.*

### Rubric

This is your first Figma assignment, so "Proficient" is the expected bar for a
good-faith attempt that applies what we covered in class. "Excellent" is for
work that goes beyond that, not the default expectation.

| Category                            | Excellent                                                       | Proficient                                                                                                   | Needs Improvement                                                   |
| ----------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Typography & hierarchy** (25 pts) | Font pairing feels intentional and reinforces the poster's tone | You picked 1–2 fonts and made *some* things bigger/bolder than others so there's a clear first thing to read | All text looks the same size/weight, or it's genuinely hard to read |
| **Layout & composition** (25 pts)   | Elements feel deliberately placed and balanced across the page  | Text and shapes are reasonably aligned (used align/distribute) and not overlapping by accident               | Elements are scattered with no attempt at alignment                 |
| **Craft & execution** (25 pts)      | Meets all technical requirements cleanly with no stray elements | Meets the technical requirements (1080×1350px, 140+ characters)                                              | Missing one or more requirements                                    |
| **Concept** (25 pts)                | The idea is memorable or shows a clear point of view            | There's a clear, identifiable subject/message                                                                | It's unclear what the poster is even for                            |

### Figma tools lecture

#### Set your frame to the exact size

1. Select the frame tool
2. In the right panel:
	1. Select Social Media -> Instagram Post
	2. OR type **1080** into **W** (width) and **1350** into **H** (height).

#### Text styles (reusable font presets)

A **text style** is a saved combination of font, size, weight, and line height
that you can apply to any text box with one click. Most posters have 3–4 kinds
of text (headline, subhead, details, body). Styles keep every piece of text of
the same kind looking identical. If you decide your body text should be
bigger, you change the style once and every body text box updates.

1. Style one text box the way you want it (e.g. your body text).
2. In the right panel, next to **Typography**, click the **style icon** (four
   dots), then **+**. Name it something clear, like "Body."
3. To use it: select another text box → click the same icon → choose "Body."
4. To edit a style: open the style list, hover over the style, and click the
   edit (sliders) icon. Every text box using it will update.

#### Alignment tools (review)

You saw align and distribute last class. Here's when to use each:

- **Align** (top of the right panel): lines up the edges or centers of
  whatever you've selected. If you select one layer by itself, it aligns to
  the frame, which is the quickest way to center a headline on the poster.
- **Distribute:** makes the gaps between three or more things equal.
- **Smart guides:** the thin red lines and numbers that appear while you drag.
  They show when something is lined up with, or the same distance from, a
  nearby element. Watch for them.

**Intentional vs. accidental misalignment.** Breaking alignment can look
great, like one word tilted, offset, or bleeding off the edge. It only works
when it's obviously on purpose. Things that are *almost* lined up (off by a few
pixels)  look like a mistake. If something isn't lined up, move it far
enough that it's clearly a choice.

#### Layout grids (something to align to)

A **layout grid** draws guide columns over your frame. They only show up in
Figma, not in your export. They give you edges to line things up against so
you're not eyeballing everything.

https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/360040450513-Create-layout-guides

1. Select your frame.
2. In the right panel, find **Layout guide** (called **Layout grid** in some
   versions of Figma) and click **+**.
3. Click the grid icon to open its settings. Change **Grid** to **Columns**,
   then set **Count: 4**, **Margin: 72**, **Gutter: 24**.
4. Now snap your text boxes and shapes to the column edges. The margin keeps
   everything a safe distance from the edge of the poster.

### Exporting

- Select the Frame your poster was created in
- At the bottom of the right panel, click **+** next to **Export**.
- Choose **PNG** or **JPG** and keep the scale at **1x**.
- Filenaming best practices
	- Add a date and a version, for example:
	- ghostinthemachine_092926_v1.jpeg
### What "good" looks like

Here's a checklist you can use on your own poster:

[ ] **Split your text into levels rather than one long sentence:**

1. **Headline:** the one thing people read first (biggest, boldest)
2. **Subhead:** a short line that explains the headline
3. **Details:** what, when, where, price, etc.
4. **Call to action:** what the viewer should do ("Get tickets," "Show up,"
   "Out now")

Each level should look clearly different from the others.

[ ] **Make size differences obvious.** 

[ ] **Match the font to the subject.** 

[ ] **Test your poster:**

- **Squint test:** blur your eyes (or zoom way out) and look at your poster.
  What do you notice first? Is that what you *want* people to notice first?
- **Phone test:** export a draft and open it on your phone. This is an
  Instagram-sized post, so that's how people would see it. If you have to
  pinch-zoom to read the small text, it's too small. At this size, body text
  under about **28px** is hard to read on a phone.

### Partner workshop

**Setup:**

1. In your poster file, click **Share** (top right) → **Copy link**, and send
   it to your partner.
2. Open your partner's link.
3. Press **C** (or click the speech-bubble icon in the toolbar), click
   directly on the part of the poster you're commenting on, type your
   comment, and press **Enter**.

**Some questions for you to discuss with your partner**

1. **Subject:** what do you think this poster is for? Is the subject clear?
2. **First read:** What did your eye land on first? Second? Is that the order your partner wants?
3. **Readability:** Is any text hard to read, whether it's too small, low
   contrast, or sitting on a busy background?
4. **Typefaces:** Are there no more than two typefaces, and does each one
   have a clear job (headline vs. everything else)?
5. **Alignment:** Is the text lined up? If something is misaligned, does it
   look intentional or accidental?
6. **Requirements:** Is the frame 1080 × 1350? Does it look like 140+
   characters?

Try and be specific. "The first thing my eye goes to is the date."
