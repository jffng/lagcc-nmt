## Assignment 3: Animation

**Due:** Tuesday, November 19, at the start of class
**Submit:** MP4 + process note on Brightspace

### Two options:

1. **Animate your Assignment 2.** Turn your comic or photo essay into a 30–60 second animation.
2. **Make something new.**  A fresh 30–60 second animation on any topic you care about.

### Requirements

- **Size:** 1080 × 1920, 30 fps
- **Length:** 30–60 seconds
- **Format:** MP4
- **Sound:** music, sound effects, or voice-over
- **Motion:** something on screen is animated (text, images, illustrations, graphics)
- **A real transition** between at least two scenes (more below)
- **A mask or wipe** somewhere in the piece
- **A process note** — 2 or 3 sentences about what you did, where your images + footage and sound came from, and one thing you built or customized that you're proud of.

### Which software?

**After Effects is the default.** It's what we've been using in class, and it's what I can help you with.

**CapCut (or another editor you already know) is fine too.** Use whatever gets you to a finished piece. The catch: I can't troubleshoot it as well, and the rubric is the same for everyone. Whichever tool you use, you still need to hit everything above. Watch out for watermarks and locked export settings, and double-check your size and frame rate before you export.

## Can I use video?

You can use video clips, but the focus is on your animation. Footage should support your piece, not carry it: every scene should have something you animated yourself, and a clip that just plays on its own won't count toward Motion or Transitions. Use your own footage or a free source, get consent from anyone who appears in it, and no AI-generated video.

### Rubric (100 points)

| Category                | Points | What I'm looking for                                                                                                                                        |
| ----------------------- | ------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Motion**              | 20     | Things move on purpose. Keyframes (or the equivalent) with easing, not just stuff appearing. Not too busy: a few well-chosen movements beat ten messy ones. |
| **Transitions & masks** | 20     | At least one real transition and one mask or wipe.                                                                                                          |
| **Sound**               | 15     | Sound fits the piece and is balanced, so nothing drowns out the rest.                                                                                       |
| **Story & pacing**      | 20     | There's a beginning, middle and end. Each scene stays up long enough to read or take in, and the length is 30–60 seconds.                                   |
| **Craft & legibility**  | 15     | Text is readable on a phone. Clean edges, no stray artifacts, correct size and frame rate, clean export.                                                    |
| **Process note**        | 10     | Describe what you did, where and how you got the footage, and one thing you built / customized that you're proud of.                                        |

If you tried something ambitious and it is not perfect, that is great. I'd rather see you try. 

## After Effects Demos

### Transitions between frames

**1. Hard cut**

- Frame 2's layers start the instant frame 1's end
- Nothing to animate. Often the best choice.

**2. Fade**

- Overlap the frames by about 0.5 seconds
- Animate Opacity (**T**) on the outgoing layers: 100% → 0%

**3. Push (slide)**

1. *Layer → New → Null Object*
2. Select all frame 1 layers and **parent** them to the null: in the **Parent & Link** column, drag the pick whip (the spiral icon) to the null
3. Animate the **null's Position** (**P**) off to the left. Everything attached moves with it.
4. Do the same for frame 2, sliding in from the right
5. **F9** both. Overlap them so one pushes the other.

**4. Wipe with an effect**

1. Select a layer (or, next class, a whole frame)
2. *Effect → Transition → Linear Wipe*
3. Animate **Transition Completion**: 0% → 100% (the layer wipes away)
4. Try different **Wipe Angle** values (0°, 90°, 180°, 270°). Find the direction you want.
5. **Feather** softens the edge
6. Ease the keyframes

### masks

**A mask is a shape that hides part of a layer.**

1. Select the layer **first**
2. Choose the **Rectangle** or **Ellipse** tool (**Q** cycles shape tools)
3. Drag on the layer in the Composition panel. Everything outside the shape disappears.
4. Double-click the tool in the toolbar to make a mask that fits the layer exactly

**Gotcha:** if no layer is selected, the same tool draws a **shape layer**, not a mask

**Show the properties**

- **M**: Mask Path
- **MM** (tap twice): all mask properties
- **Mask Feather** softens the edge
- **Mask Expansion** grows or shrinks it

**Animate a mask (reveal)**

1. Draw a rectangle mask over a title, tiny to start
2. At 0:00, click the stopwatch on **Mask Path**
3. Move to 0:20 (20 frames). Switch to the Selection tool (**V**), double-click the mask, and drag it to cover the whole title.
4. **F9**
5. The title is now revealed by a moving window

### Demo: track mattes (wipes with shapes)

**A matte uses one layer as the cut-out shape for another.**

1. Draw a **shape layer** (nothing selected, Rectangle tool) above your image layer
2. Press **F4** to show the Switches/Modes columns
3. On the image layer, open the **TrkMat** dropdown → **Alpha Matte** and pick the shape layer
4. The shape layer's eye turns off automatically
5. Now animate the **shape's Scale or Position**: the image appears only inside the shape

**Wipe recipes**

- **Iris wipe:** circle matte, Scale 0% → 150%
- **Slide wipe:** rectangle matte, Position from off-screen to centered
- **Reveal from a line:** rectangle matte on a text layer, animate the text's Position up from below the matte's edge
