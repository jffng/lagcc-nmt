### Lecture: Typography, continued

We'll finish covering the core properties of typography — font families, size,
kerning (spacing between letters), and line height — and this time, see how to
actually control each of these in Figma: the font family dropdown, size, weight,
line height, letter spacing, alignment, and color.

We'll also cover two things you'll need for the poster assignment:

- **Choosing fonts**: stick to one or two font families max. Pair one
  expressive/display font for a headline with one plain, easy-to-read font for
  body text — or just use different weights of the same font if you want to keep
  it simple.
- **Building hierarchy**: decide what you want people to read first, second, and
  third, and use size, weight, and color contrast to make that order obvious.
  Not everything on the page can be the loudest thing. We'll also look at the
  **layers panel**, which controls what's stacked on top of what — useful once
  text and shapes start to overlap.

### Shape tools & layout in Figma

A few more tools that'll help you build your poster:

- **Shapes** (rectangle, ellipse) — useful for color blocks or backgrounds
  behind your text
- **Fill** — the color or image inside a shape
- **Opacity** — how see-through something is
- **Effects** — drop shadow and blur are the two most useful for a poster
- **Align & distribute** — keep elements consistently lined up instead of
  eyeballing it
- **Grouping** — combine related elements (like text + its background shape) so
  they move and resize together
