### Lecture: Typography, continued

We'll finish covering the core properties of typography — font families, size,
kerning (spacing between letters), and line height — and this time, see how to
actually control each of these in Figma: the font family dropdown, size, weight,
line height, letter spacing, alignment, and color.

We'll also cover two things you'll need for the poster assignment:

- **Choosing fonts**: stick to one or two font families max. Pair one
  expressive/display font for a headline with one plain, easy-to-read font for
  body text — or just use different weights of the same font if you want to keep
  it simple.
- **Building hierarchy**: decide what you want people to read first, second, and
  third, and use size, weight, and color contrast to make that order obvious.
  Not everything on the page can be the loudest thing. We'll also look at the
  **layers panel**, which controls what's stacked on top of what — useful once
  text and shapes start to overlap.

### Shape tools & layout in Figma

A few more tools that'll help you build your poster. We'll build a simplified
version of the poster above, live, using only what's covered here:

- **Shapes** (rectangle, ellipse) — useful for color blocks or backgrounds
  behind your text. We'll also rotate a square to fake a triangle, rather than
  introducing the vector/pen tool today.
- **Fill** — the color or image inside a shape
- **Opacity** — how see-through something is
- **Effects** — drop shadow and blur are the two most useful for a poster
- **Align & distribute** — keep elements consistently lined up instead of
  eyeballing it
- **Grouping** — combine related elements (like text + its background shape) so
  they move and resize together

**Demo plan:** build a stripped-down version of the poster above — a repeated
headline (stacked manually with copy-paste, not auto layout), the three
geometric shapes, fill + opacity, and align/distribute for the address/details
block. We'll skip auto layout, the vector tool, and the glitch effect for now —
those are tools for later once the basics are second nature.

### Assignment: Poster

**Due Thursday, Oct 1**

Make a poster. It can be about anything — an event, a comic, a game, a product, a business. 

***Requirements***
- 1080 × 1350 px (Instagram's portrait post size)
- Must contain at least 140 characters of text
- Can contain some kind of photography or imagery, but it's not required. The focus is on the text

*Examples*
- Remix an album cover
- Promote a concert
- Protest flyer
- Game or movie cover

*Submit via Brightspace.*

### Rubric

| Category | Excellent | Proficient | Needs Improvement |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Typography & hierarchy** (25 pts) | Font choice fits the poster's tone; clear order of what to read first, second, third through size/weight/color | Fonts are readable but hierarchy is unclear, or too many competing focal points | No clear reading order; text is hard to read or fonts clash without purpose |
| **Layout & composition** (25 pts) | Elements are aligned and balanced; whitespace is used intentionally | Layout is functional but inconsistent spacing/alignment | Elements feel randomly placed; cramped or unbalanced |
| **Craft & execution** (25 pts) | Meets all technical requirements (1080×1350px, 140+ characters) cleanly; no stray/misaligned elements | Meets requirements but with rough edges (sloppy alignment, inconsistent spacing) | Missing a requirement, or execution is visibly unfinished |
| **Concept & effectiveness** (25 pts) | The poster clearly communicates its subject/message and would work as a real poster for its stated purpose | Concept is present but message or purpose is unclear at a glance | No clear concept, or the poster doesn't communicate what it's for |
