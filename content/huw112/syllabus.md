## **Course Description**

This course introduces students to the field of new media technology. For our purposes, new media are forms of communication and expression made with computers. We will study and practice various foundational modes of digital media: text, digital graphics, videos, and websites. 

This is both a lecture and studio class, serving as the foundational course for the New Media Technology major at LAGCC. Most classes will contain some lecture, followed by some time to practice and work on your assignments and projects. We will learn together and share what we learn. 

By the end of this course students will have a deeper understanding of the role that computational media plays in how we communicate, in addition to practical skills that allow you apply them.

## **Goals**

The course has three goals:

1. to introduce you to the field of new media.
2. to equip you with hands-on skills to realize your ideas.
3. to help you develop your own critical and creative understanding of new media, and figure out which media you enjoy working with and want to pursue further.

## **Objectives**

On completion of this course, you will be able to:

1. Lay out text using a computer.
2. Create and manipulate digital images.
3. Create videos and animations.
4. Code a basic website by hand with HTML and CSS.
5. Explain your reasons for using a digital tool.
6. Showcase projects you made in class via an online portfolio.

*Having objectives allows us to evaluate how well we have achieved our goals.*

***Is this course for me?***

This course is relevant to students who are interested in:

- Using computers for artistic, expressive, creative purposes
- Designing interactive experiences including websites, games, or other creative environments
- Understanding the critical aspects of computing in culture today

***What this course is NOT:***

- A purely theoretical or philosophical study of new media
- A deep dive into Adobe, coding, or any one specific way of making
- Using generative AI to entirely create images and videos

## Course Overview

### Unit 1 — Text

*September 15 – 29*

An introduction to the field of new media and to the tools and habits we will use all semester. 

We will look at text as the foundational method of interacting with computers and each other.

**Tools:** macOS (Finder, Spotlight), TextEdit, VS Code, terminal

***

### Unit 2 — Digital Images

*Oct 1 — 22*

Creating, manipulating images, posters, and memes digitally. 

**Tools:** Adobe Illustrator, Figma

***

### Unit 3 — Video & Animation

*Oct 27 — Nov 17*

How is a video different than a movie? What happens when everyone has a video camera?  How do sounds affect us compared to visuals?  

We will look at how to create and manipulate time-based media — how to create and edit text, images, and sounds in combination to create video and audio. 

**Tools:** Adobe Premiere  

***

### Unit 4 — Website

*Nov 19 — Dec 3*

What is a website made of? Is Instagram a website? What happens when suddenly billions of people across the world can talk to each other instantly via the Internet?

We will look at the Internet, and how people put things there, and what is possible.

**Tools:** VS Code, HTML, CSS

***

### Final Projects & Presentations

*Dec 8 — 10*

You will take what you have learned and combine it to create a final project. 

***

## **Grading Policy** 

Letter grades are determined by a combination of your work and participation in class:

40% assignments

30% final project and portfolio

30% participation (includes attendance)

Assignments are evaluated according to effort, completeness, and creativity you put in. Late work is always accepted, but will receive one letter grade down per late class:

- 1 class late — one grade down A→B 
- 2 classes late — two grades down A→C
- 3 classes late - D

**Final grade examples:**

A — Completes every assignment and final project with great effort and creative enthusiasm. Active and consistent participant during class time.

B — Assignments completed with few late submissions, and the required effort. You are attendant and participate in class discussions and work.

C — Frequent late work. Assignments are submitted but show inconsistent effort. Participation is limited.

D — Significant missing or late work. Incomplete participation, or inconsistent and / or disruptive effort.

F — Failure to submit required work, attendance and participation are non-existent or extremely disruptive. 

**Attendance Policy**

Your on-time attendance is crucial. Roll call is within 5 - 10 minutes of the beginning of class. You are expected to arrive on time and remain for the entire class period. Take a break when you need it, you don't need to ask, just try to not disrupt others. 

- 10 minutes late = 1 late
- 30 minutes late or leaving class for more than 30 minutes = 1 absence
- 3 lates = 1 absence
- 5 absences = Automatic withdrawal (WD)
- Final critique absence = F

If you are absent, you are responsible for finding and reviewing information and homework from that class, via Brightspace. 

- Absences without communicating with me beforehand count toward your 4 absence limit.
- If you miss class without communication, it is your responsibility to figure out what occurred during that session
- It is not my to chase students to review missed material or submit late assignments.

For **excused absences**:

- It is your responsibility to seek out the information that was covered in class. You are more than welcome to send me a message and I will help point you to the resources.
- It is your responsibility to complete any assignments due that day.
- You are encouraged to come to office hours or message me for extra help.

**Participation**

Attendance does not equal participation. I look forward to sharing work, and discussing with you and learning together. We spend so much time staring at our phones. I ask that while I or one of your fellow students is speaking, to listen intently. 
 
**Use of Artificial Intelligence (AI)**

This class is focused on developing your critical media literacy and technical abilities. Submitting work that is **primarily** or **entirely** **generated** **by** **AI** will be considered a failure to complete the assignment. If AI is used to create an entire assignment or project, the result will be an automatic 0. Repeated misuse of AI will result in a failing grade for the course.
