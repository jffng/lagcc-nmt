### Unit 3: Video & Animation

Unit 3 introduces **time**.

**Animation is a series of images, played in sequence.** That's also what your carousel is: first this, then this, then this. There is one big difference.

- **In a carousel, the reader sets the pace.** They swipe when they're ready.
- **In a video, you set the pace.** You decide what appears first, how long they look at it, and what comes next. If it's too fast, they miss it. If it's too slow, they leave.

Look back at the board. Everything on it made someone decide what came first, how long it lasted, and what you heard. That's what this unit is about.

We'll use **Adobe After Effects**.

Your photo essay or comic is the starting material, so everyone has something to work with. If something on the board is more exciting to you, we can go that way too.

### Plan your video: pre-storyboard

On paper or in Notes, list your frames in order. Next to each one write:

- **How many seconds it stays on screen** (aim for 3–4 seconds each, 20–30 total)
- **What appears first** on that frame (the title? the photo? a face?)
- **How it ends** (a cut? a slide? something else)

This is your first storyboard. You will change it.



### Prepare your carousel for After Effects

After Effects can't animate a flattened picture. If your whole frame is one image, you can only move the whole frame. To make things move separately, each thing needs to be its own file.

**1. Name your layers**

Rename every layer you want to animate: `photo`, `title`, `bubble-1`, `caption`. Not `Rectangle 47`. After Effects will show you these names, and you'll thank yourself.

**2. Separate what moves from what stays**

Ask: what could move on this frame? Usually the background, the main image, the text, and any decorations. Pull each one out into its own layer.

**3. Export each layer on its own**

- Select a layer
- In the right panel, find **Export** and click **+**
- **Photos and drawings:** PNG, **2x**
- **Shapes, speech bubbles, simple graphics:** SVG
- Click **Export**

You can select several layers and export them all at once. Each becomes its own file.

**4. Put the files in folders**

```
My Video/
  frame-1/
    background.png
    photo.png
    bubble-1.svg
  frame-2/
  ...
```

**5. Check the size**

Your frames should already be **1080 × 1920** or **1080 × 1350**. Write yours down. We'll use it to set up After Effects.

**6. Leave the text as text**

Don't export your words as images. We'll retype them in After Effects so they can be animated.
