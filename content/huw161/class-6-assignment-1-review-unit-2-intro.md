### Unit 2 intro: designing for someone else

For the next five weeks, you'll design for **a classmate**.

#### What you'll make

A **landing page** for your partner: a single web page with one main job. It
introduces something and gets a visitor to do one thing, like sign up, visit,
buy, or get in touch. Your partner is your **client**, and their project
(a side hustle, club, hobby, family business, cause) is what the page is
about. At the same time, you'll be the client for their page.

- **Wireframe checkpoint:** Tuesday, Oct 27. A **wireframe** is a grayscale
  version of the page that shows structure only: what goes where and what's
  most important, with no color or styling yet.
- **Final landing page:** Thursday, Nov 5. Desktop and mobile versions, with
  color and images.

#### What that means from a UX perspective

**UX (user experience) design** is designing around what a real person needs
to do, not only around how something looks. Designing for a client changes
how you work in a few ways:

- **You are not the user.** Your taste matters less than what your client's
  visitors need. You'll find that out by **asking**: next class, you'll
  interview your client.
- **One page, one goal.** A landing page succeeds if a visitor knows what it
  is and what to do within a few seconds.
- **Structure before style.** You'll build the wireframe in grayscale first.
- **Hierarchy is how the goal gets across.** **Visual hierarchy** is the
  order in which someone notices things: what they see first, second,
  third. You practiced this with type in Assignment 1, where headline vs.
  body vs. label was a hierarchy. Now it applies to the whole page.
- **Most visitors are on a phone.** 
- **You'll test it with real people.**
### Matchmaking Board

Your partner for the next five weeks will be matched by **shared interests**.
If your client's project is in a world you care about, you'll understand
their visitors better and make better design decisions.

Open the [Pair Matching board](https://www.figma.com/board/iVVoVLTHG3vYi8pHMAKaEP/Pair-matching?node-id=0-1&p=f&t=JlMYXQS8LMPXrc9H-0) and make a copy of the blue, yellow, green, and purple stickies.

#### 1. Fill in your card (silent)

Each color of sticky is a different question:

- 🟨 **Something in my life that could use a website.** A side hustle, a
  club, a hobby, a family business, a band, an event, a cause, a favorite
  local spot. If nothing comes to mind, make one up: the business or club
  you'd start if you could.
- 🟦 **Three things I could talk about for an hour.** One per sticky.
- 🟩 **Who would visit that website, and what do they need from it?** One
  sentence.
- 🟪 *(Optional)* **I'd rather get feedback…** in person, in writing, or a mix.

#### 2. Gallery walk

Read everyone's card, note to yourself which ones sound interesting.

Rank your top three.
#### 3. Cluster the interests

Copy everyone's 🟦 stickies into the **Clusters** area, and as a class, drag
them into groups (music, food, sports, gaming, family, community, etc.). Give
each group a name.

This is called **affinity mapping**. Designers use it to sort lots of
research notes into patterns. You'll use it again in your final project.
