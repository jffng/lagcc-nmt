### Opportunity statement

Turn your interview into one clear sentence about what your user needs. Leave the solution out — that comes next.

**Fill in:**

> [Name] wants [their idea] so that [outcome], but [obstacle].
> How might we help [Name] [outcome as a verb]?

**Three moves:**

1. **Ask "so that…?" twice.** I want a portfolio → so people can see my illustrations → so I can get paid commissions. Stop while it's still about this person.
2. **Turn the noun into a verb.** Portfolio → show my work to the right people. DJ app → practice mixing.
3. **Name three solutions.** If only one solution fits your statement, climb one more "so that."

**Examples:**

- Rio wants a portfolio so that she can get paid commissions, but people who like her art don't know how to hire her.
  → How might we help Rio turn people who like her art into paying clients?
- Jeff wants to record recipes while he cooks, but his hands are busy and the details get lost.
  → How might we help Jeff capture a recipe in the moment?
- Jeff wants to practice DJing, but he can't afford the equipment.
  → How might we help Jeff build DJ skills before he owns any gear?

**Check:**

- ❌ Names a solution: "How might we make a birthday card app?"
- ❌ Too broad: "How might we help people be happier?"
- ✅ Names the person, uses something they said, leaves the solution open

Already have an idea? Keep it. The statement tells you what your idea has to *do* — and that's your landing page's main button.
