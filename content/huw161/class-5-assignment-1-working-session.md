### Assignment: Recreate & Reimagine

**Due Thursday, October 1**

1. Take one of the examples you pulled from the Website Scavenger Hunt.

2. Recreate it in Figma — match the original's layout and text hierarchy as
   closely as you can (what's the headline, what's body text, what's a button
   or label).

3. Reimagine it using **three different typeface options**. All three must
   live in a **single Figma frame**, laid out so they can be compared side by
   side.

4. For each of your three options, write a short explanation (1–2 sentences)
   of *why* you chose that typeface — how it changes the tone, legibility, or
   feeling of the site compared to the original.

**Requirements**
- One Figma frame containing the recreated layout and all three typeface
  options
- Each option must actually change the typeface, not just the size or color
- A short written rationale per option, referencing at least one concept from
  class (hierarchy, weight, kerning, line height, or serif vs. sans-serif)
- Export your frame PNG or JPEGs, and submit it along with a link
  to your Figma file.

**Submit via Brightspace.**

### Figma demo: text styles and layout grids

Both tools today are **optional**

#### Text styles (only if you haven't duplicated yet)

A **text style** is a saved preset of font, size, weight, and line height.
You apply it to a text box with one click. If you edit the style later, every
text box using it updates.

**Why use it here:** each of your options has 3–4 levels of text (headline,
body, button label, etc.). With styles, you can change one option's
headline font in a single edit instead of clicking every headline one by
one.

**The trap to avoid:** if all four copies share one "Headline" style,
changing its font changes **all four at once**, and your options stop being
different. Each version needs its own set of styles. Name them with a slash,
and Figma will group them into folders:

- `Original/Headline`, `Original/Body`, `Original/Label`
- `Option 1/Headline`, `Option 1/Body`, `Option 1/Label`
- …and the same for Options 2 and 3

**How:**

1. In your recreation, style one headline the way it looks on the original
   site.
2. In the right panel, next to **Typography**, click the **style icon** (four
   dots), then **+**. Name it `Original/Headline`.
3. Repeat for body text and labels, then apply each style to the matching
   text in your recreation. Select a text box → style icon → pick the style.
4. Now duplicate your recreation (**⌘/Ctrl + D**). The copy still uses the
   `Original` styles.
5. In the copy, change one headline's font. Figma will show the style as
   modified. Click **+** to save it as a new style named `Option 1/Headline`,
   then apply that style to the rest of the headlines in that copy.
6. Repeat for each level of text and each option.

From then on, any tweak to an option's size or line height happens in one
place and stays consistent throughout that option.

#### Layout grids

A **layout grid** draws guide columns over a frame. You see them only while
you work in Figma, not in your export. Most websites are built on a grid of
12 columns, so adding one to your recreation helps you match the original's
structure.

1. Select your recreation frame.
2. In the right panel, find **Layout guide** (called **Layout grid** in some
   versions) and click **+**.
3. Click the grid icon to open its settings. Set **Grid** to **Columns** and
   **Count** to **12**.
4. With the original screenshot visible underneath, adjust **Margin** until
   the outer columns line up with the edges of the site's content, and
   adjust **Gutter** (the gap between columns) until the columns line up with
   the site's content blocks.
5. Snap your text boxes and images to the column edges.

**Already past the recreation?** Use **Align** and **Distribute** on the four
versions and their caption boxes so they're evenly spaced and lined up
across the parent frame. That's graded under **Craft & execution**.

### Partner check-in

Pair up and swap Figma links: **Share** (top right) → **Copy link**. Open
your partner's file and leave **Figma comments** directly on their work.
Press **C**, click on the spot you're commenting on, type, and press
**Enter**.

Leave at least one comment for each question:

1. **Recreation:** Compare their recreation to the original screenshot. Is
   the headline / body / label hierarchy the same? Point out anything that's
   off.
2. **Typefaces:** Are the three options clearly different typefaces, not
   just different sizes? Which option fits the site's purpose best, and why?
3. **Rationale:** Read one of their explanations. Does it name a class
   concept (hierarchy, weight, kerning, line height, serif vs. sans-serif)
   and say what actually changes? Or is it closer to "it looks nice"?
4. **At a glance:** Zoom out until you can see the whole parent frame. Is it
   easy to tell which version is which? Is everything aligned and evenly
   spaced?

Be specific. "The body text in Option 2 is so light it's hard to read" is
useful. "Looks good!" isn't.
